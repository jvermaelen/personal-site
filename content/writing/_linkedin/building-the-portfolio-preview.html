Spent the last few weeks building jasonvermaelen.com. Two reasons. One: a place to house past project writeups and future projects I take on. Two: a real-world test of working alongside AI tools - what does it actually look like when you build something real with Claude as the pair-programmer. The site itself is the experiment. The writeups (live at /work) are the deliverable. A few build decisions that mattered: → One accent color, full stop. Single cobalt on off-white. If something doesn't use the accent, it had better be structural. Forces every element to earn its place. → Mandatory tradeoffs sections. Every project writeup has a "what I'd do differently" beat. The signal lives there. It separates someone who executed a plan from someone who learned something from it. → Claude as a collaborator. Claude Code did the iteration on the build; Claude Design (claude.ai/design) helped me work through the PRD before any of it shipped. The design choices were mine. The iteration speed wasn't. That meta-experiment of working with AI is part of what the site is. What surprised me most: writing the project deep-dives took way longer than building the site itself. The deliverables run slower than the scaffold. Worth knowing if you're thinking about a similar setup. More on the rejected directions, the design decisions, and the one thing I'd tell anyone else thinking about a similar project - in the build log on the site.
